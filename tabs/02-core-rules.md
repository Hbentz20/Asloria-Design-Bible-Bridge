# Core Rules

Core resolution, skills and tools, and the Attribute Expressions. The full d100 skill system is in the Skill Tests tab.

## Core resolution

The game uses two dice (Established 2026-10-13). Attacks and initiative roll a d20 against Defense, and saves roll a d20 against a DC. Skill checks are d100 tests: roll equal to or under a percentage (see the Skill Tests tab). Saves moved to d100 on 2026-10-13 and returned to the d20 on 2026-10-14, so that no single action asks for two kinds of dice. Other subsystems may still use other dice where they fit; see the decision log. (Established)

**Attributes (Established)**

**Attribute names (Established 2026-10-14):** Might (Strength), Acuity (Perception), Vigor (Endurance), Presence (Charisma), Sagacity (Intelligence), Poise (Agility), Providence (Luck). These are final. Alternatives stay in the Player Feedback Log (entry 1) as optional feedback, not an open decision. The S.P.E.C.I.A.L. acronym is retired with the old names.

| Attribute | Governs | Expression | Saves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Might (formerly Strength) | Lifting, pushing, melee blows, athletics | Momentum | Fortitude, Footing |
| Acuity (formerly Perception) | All five senses, noticing danger, aptitude for critical hits | Focus | Reflex, Scrutiny |
| Vigor (formerly Endurance) | Stamina, durability, resisting toxins | Grit | Fortitude, Courage |
| Presence (formerly Charisma) | Social force; one of two magic attributes (wild and pact magic) | Authority | Will, Courage |
| Sagacity (formerly Intelligence) | Knowledge, languages, problem solving; black magic, alchemy | Cognition | Will, Scrutiny |
| Poise (formerly Agility) | Finesse, accuracy, coordination, ranged weapons, trickery | Tempo | Reflex, Footing |
| Providence (formerly Luck) | Subverting the dice; a mostly finite resource; can substitute for other attributes when specified | Fortune | Substitutes for one attribute in any save |

**Attribute scale rebuild (Established direction 2026-10-14):** attributes move to a 0–100 scale. Scores are mainly used to derive other values (modifiers, skill talent, saves, pools), allowing gradual progress players can see and feel. XP alone can raise an attribute only up to a ceiling; going beyond it requires narrative rewards, gear, magic or other in-world means. The conversion, starting scores and XP ceiling are set below; the old 1–10 rules were removed 2026-10-14.

**Score to modifier (Established 2026-10-14):** 50 is an ordinary adult (+0). Above 50, +1 for every 5 points; below 50, −1 for every 10 points, rounding toward zero. Gear, path abilities or magic can add the last +1 to reach +11. **XP ceiling: 75** (+5); scores above 75 come only from narrative rewards, gear, magic or other in-world means. Attributes never exceed 100.

| Score | 0 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 55 | 60 | 70 | 75 | 80 | 90 | 100 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modifier | −5 | −3 | −2 | −1 | +0 | +1 | +2 | +4 | +5 | +6 | +8 | +10 |

**Starting scores (Established 2026-10-14):** all seven attributes start at 40. Apply race first: +5, +5, −5. Then spend 30 points to raise attributes, gaining 1 point for each point lowered. Spend in steps of 5 at creation. After race, every attribute must be between 25 and 60. Every character totals 310 before race (315 after). Quick-start array: 60, 50, 45, 40, 40, 40, 35. After creation, attributes grow a point or a few at a time through XP (to 75) and in-world means (beyond 75).

**Fit with existing rules (to recheck):** a new character's best attribute is 60 (+2), which matches the +2 attack modifier the Guard simulations assumed; rerun them once monster numbers use the same scale. Grit and Focus equal the Vigor and Acuity tens digits, so a new character holds 2 to 6 of each (Established 2026-10-17), and each recovers 1 point on a successful Short Rest d100 roll. New-character save bonuses range from −4 to +4; the quick-start array gives −2 to +2. Skill talent converts as (A + B) ÷ 5 (Established 2026-10-15; see the Skill Tests tab), so a trained skill starts at 30–44%.

**Saving throws (Established)**

| Save | Use | Attributes (A + B) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reflex | Avoid and evade: traps, area effects, sudden danger | Poise + Acuity |
| Fortitude | Withstand and weather: poison, disease, fatigue, pain | Might + Vigor |
| Will | Resist: magic, mind manipulation, domination | Presence + Sagacity |
| Footing | Keep your feet: shoves, trips, knockdowns, forced movement | Might + Poise |
| Courage | Hold firm against fear, horror, intimidation | Vigor + Presence |
| Scrutiny | See through: illusions, lies, hidden things, deception | Acuity + Sagacity |

**Providence in saves (Established):** when making a save, you may substitute Providence for one of its two attributes. You can do this a number of times equal to your Providence modifier per Long Rest, to a minimum of 0: a modifier of 0 or below gives no uses (minimum Established 2026-10-24).

**Save formula (Established 2026-10-14):** saves roll d20 against a DC. Save bonus = modifier A + modifier B (from the 0–100 conversion), to a maximum of +7 from attributes. Proposed: relics, path abilities and magic may add up to +3 beyond that. Providence substitution still applies. Six saves (Reflex, Fortitude, Will, Footing, Courage, Scrutiny); each attribute appears in exactly two saves. Primary names are final for now (Established 2026-10-14); alternatives are logged on the Player Feedback Log. Attribute pairings are Established (2026-10-14). Save DCs are set by the rule below.

**Save DC (Established 2026-10-15):** DC = 10 + attribute modifier A + attribute modifier B (to a maximum of +7) + Potency (0 to +3). Every ability that forces a save names its two DC attributes, just as every save and skill names its two. Potency comes from the ability itself, relics or magic; most abilities have Potency 0, and how Potency grows will be set with the XP economy. When the attacker's pair equals the defender's pair, the defender saves 55% of the time at every stage of play. There is no automatic success or failure on a natural 20 or natural 1 (rejected 2026-10-15; logged on the Player Feedback Log for later).

**Damage terms (Established)**

- **DR X**: flat reduction from any source. 6 damage against DR 2 means 4 taken.
- **Resistance (type)**: that damage type is halved.

**Encounters and rests (Established 2026-10-16; numbers Draft):**

|  | What it is | What comes back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End of an encounter | An encounter starts when initiative is rolled and ends when no hostile creature is still fighting and the survivors have a moment to breathe (GM's call) | Guard returns to full; once-per-encounter uses (Rally, Stay Standing) reset |
| Brief Rest | 10 minutes of quiet, no travel or fighting. At most 2 Brief Rests before you need a Short Rest | Brief-Rest abilities recharge. One Tend per wounded character: a successful Medicine test restores 1d4 HP, at most once per patient per day |
| Short Rest | A night's sleep (6–8 hours) somewhere relatively safe; once per day | Regain HP equal to half your Vigor's tens digit, rounded down (minimum 1); make the morning d100 recovery rolls for Grit and Focus; Short-Rest abilities recharge; Brief Rests become available again |
| Long Rest | A week or more of light tasks in safety | HP to full; every Expression pool refills; Long-Rest abilities recharge; Injuries progress toward healing with treatment; Clarity can recover with the right support |

An interrupted rest gives nothing, except that a Short Rest broken by a brief fight can be finished afterward. Ability cadence: "once per Brief Rest" is roughly once per fight with a breather between, "once per Short Rest" is once per day, "per Long Rest" is per week. A Short Rest is 8 hours (Established 2026-10-21). Going 14 Short Rests without a Long Rest carries negative effects (Established 2026-10-21; the effects are Open). Audit item: about eight abilities currently "once per Short Rest" (Visage of Frenzy, Spur to Strike, Call to Stand, No Step Back, Sunderer of Sigils, Revenant Elegy, Eldritch Mind, Gnome Cunning) and every "uses per Long Rest" ability need their cadence checked; some may fit better as once per encounter.

**Hazard DC ladder (Established 2026-10-15):** 10 Minor, 13 Serious, 16 Grave, 19 Deadly. Used for saves against hazards that do not come from a creature: traps, poisons, storms, curses. It is sized to the save DC formula, so a Grave hazard hits like a seasoned attacker. Replaces the 10/15/20/25/30 ladder, which was sized for d20 skill checks.

**Skill difficulty (Established 2026-10-14):** skill tests scale the character's full skill by tier: Normal full, Medium one half, Hard one third, Very Hard one fifth, rounded down (see the Skill Tests tab for the chart). This replaces the additive ladder (Easy +25% to Near Impossible −75%) adopted 2026-10-13. Specializations add to the full skill before the fraction. Tools, conditions and help change the tier instead of the percentage; Mental Wall and In the Zone are the only other situational changes to the number. Save DCs use the save DC formula and the hazard ladder.

**Advancement (Established 2026-10-14):** there are no character levels. Characters earn XP into a pool and spend it à la carte on what they want to improve. The XP economy is still to be designed. This supersedes the earlier 1–20 level range; every rule that refers to a level must be converted.

## Attribute Expressions

Each attribute grants an Expression that changes how a character plays: five are spendable resources and two are passive limits. The category name "Attribute Expressions" is Established; only Authority is final.

| Expression | Attribute | Type | Size | Core identity | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Momentum | Might | Generated, lost when pressure breaks | Max = Might tens digit − 2 (Established 2026-10-18) | Pressure: "I seize control" | Draft |
| Focus | Acuity | Static pool | = Acuity tens digit; refills on a Long Rest, and a Short Rest d100 roll equal to or under Acuity regains 1 (Established 2026-10-17) | Precision: "I find the opening" | Draft |
| Grit | Vigor | Static pool | = Vigor tens digit; refills on a Long Rest, and a Short Rest d100 roll equal to or under Vigor regains 1 (Established 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17) | Survival: "I refuse to fall" | Draft |
| Tempo | Poise | Generated, cyclical | Max = Poise mod + 3 (proposed) | Flow: "never where you expect" | Draft |
| Fortune | Providence | Static pool | = Providence mod, minimum 0 (Established 2026-10-24), refresh on Long Rest | Fate: "I tempt fate" | Draft |
| Cognition | Sagacity | Passive capacity | Archetype base + Sagacity mod | Complexity managed at once | Draft |
| Authority | Presence | Passive scope | Archetype base + Presence mod | "How much power obeys you" | Established |

### Momentum (Might)

Gain 1 when you hit with a melee attack, knock a creature prone, push a creature, reduce one to half HP, or reduce one to 0 HP; no more than once per turn from an attack. Lose all Momentum when combat ends, you are Incapacitated, or an effect says so. Proposed: lose 1 if you spend a turn with no offensive action.

**Creator's latest Momentum draft (Draft, supplied 2026-10-17; supersedes the paragraph above).** Momentum is reserved for the strong-headed, the aggressors, the game changers: those who create an advantage and push it. It is generated by an aggressive, close-quartered game. Maximum = Might modifier + 3 (written as Strength). Generate from one source, once per turn: successful grapple 1, successful attack 1, reduce a creature to 0 HP 2, knock a creature Prone 1 (bloodying a creature was cut on 2026-10-17), successfully shove a creature 1. Lose 1 for each turn you don't attempt a Momentum-generating action; lose all when Incapacitated or when combat ends. Uses: Crushing Blows (1: +1 damage before rolling; revised and Established 2026-10-18: at most 1 Momentum per attack, and the +1 applies to Guard damage only); Driving Strike (after a melee hit, push the target 5 ft per Momentum; no cap on Momentum spent, Established 2026-10-17; safeguards Established 2026-10-17: driven into a wall, obstacle or creature, both take Guard damage equal to the Momentum spent; driven toward a fall, fire or other deadly hazard, the target makes a Footing save and on a success stops at the edge, Prone; a creature larger than you moves 5 ft per 2 Momentum); Relentless Advance (2: move up to 5 ft and make a melee attack); Juggernaut (2: move at least 10 ft and Charge); Unbound (4: end Restrained).

**Momentum in the engine (Proposed findings, 2026-10-17):** each fighter gains about 4 Momentum per duel. In a mirror where only one side spends it, Crushing Blows wins 53–58%, Relentless Advance (an extra attack) 55–59%, both 55–60%; Relentless Advance at 3 Momentum drops to 53%; Crushing Blows applied only to Guard damage gives 53–56%. When everyone uses Momentum, matchups barely move except Might-poor builds (dagger Skirmisher 39% to 35%, since Might 40 gives a maximum of 1), and fights shorten: Duelist mirror 6.0 to 5.5 rounds, Defender 6.7 to 6.1. Momentum is the strongest Expression so far, and it pushes fights further below the 7-round target, which matters for the HP limit.

**Doctrines dissolved into à la carte abilities (Established 2026-10-21).** Momentum Doctrines are no longer chosen as packages. Their gain triggers, Holds and Releases become individual Momentum abilities placed in the Path skill trees, often on different branches, and any of them can be freely combined. There is no Doctrine choice at character creation, no limit on mixing abilities from different former Doctrines, and no limit on how many Holds are active at once. Balance comes only from each ability's cost and prerequisites, including its position in the tree. Each Hold keeps its threshold (Hold I: 2 Momentum; Hold II: 4 Momentum and Might 70). This supersedes the Doctrine choice at creation, the dual-Doctrine exclusion, the one cross-Doctrine ability at 2 repertoire slots, the Might 60 requirement to choose a Doctrine, and the Brawler Doctrine granting Seize from creation. Gains are additive (Established 2026-10-21): everyone with Momentum keeps the universal gain list, and each gain-trigger ability adds another way to gain, within the 3-per-round cap, so extra triggers make Momentum more reliable in a style of play without adding volume; this supersedes 'Doctrine users gain only from their Doctrine's triggers.' Creator's intent: Momentum is meant to be gained and transferred, not hoarded. Still Open: each ability's cost, Might prerequisite and tree placement; whether the Doctrine names survive as suggested starting kits; and whether Seize and Pin keep their Might 60 and 70 gates. The Doctrine table below is now a pool of ability drafts, not a list of packages.

**Momentum Expression reverted to a universal core (Established 2026-10-21).** The Momentum Expression itself is now only the pool, the universal gain and loss rules, three universal uses, and one universal Crest Release. Everything else that spends or generates Momentum, including every former Doctrine gain trigger, Hold and Release, becomes a Path ability that uses Momentum. Universal uses (Established 2026-10-21): **Crushing Blows** (1: on a hit, +1 Guard damage; at most 1 Momentum per attack; Guard damage only); **Driving Strike** (after a melee hit, push the target 5 ft per Momentum, no cap, with its established collision, edge and size safeguards); **Shoulder the Blow** (1: reduce the Guard damage from one attack against you by 1; at most 1 Momentum per attack). Relentless Advance (2: move up to 5 ft and make a melee attack) moves into the Path trees, and Juggernaut (2: move at least 10 ft and Charge) is merged into the Sword path's Break the Line; Relentless Advance is limited to once per turn wherever it sits. Unbound is deleted from the game. Follow-up: once Momentum is done, test whether the same model (small universal core plus Path abilities) fits Tempo and the other Expressions.

**Crest (Established 2026-10-21; needs sims against the 7-round target).** When you end your turn at your Momentum maximum, you are Cresting. If you are still at maximum when your next turn ends, the pressure breaks and you drop to 2 Momentum. Crest Releases are usable only while Cresting and spend all Momentum above 2. Universal Crest Release, **Overwhelm** (working name; in the Player Feedback vote, entry 5): for every 2 Momentum spent, choose one: your next hit this turn deals +1 Guard damage, or you regain 1 Guard; choices can be split across pairs. It pays at half rate so that Path Crest Releases can pay a full point per point and are worth buying. Path Crest Releases may fill the 'Hold at maximum later through the ability tree' slot.

**Momentum gain cap per round (Established 2026-10-21).** A character can gain at most 3 Momentum per round, and those 3 must come from at least 2 separate triggers, so no single trigger can supply all 3. This applies to every source, on or off your turn, including passive off-turn triggers and reaction attacks. Standfast keeps its opportunity attacks that don't use your reaction, since its uses are limited; the round cap bounds its gains. One event can pay up to 2 triggers (Established 2026-10-21), since Guard can be broken by means other than an attack. The round cap replaces the older once-per-turn gain limit (Established 2026-10-21).

**Momentum structure, decisions so far (2026-10-18; Doctrine choice, exclusion and cross-Doctrine rules superseded 2026-10-21, see above).** Established: Momentum has a universal core (the creator's five uses: Crushing Blows known by every Might user; Driving Strike, Juggernaut, Relentless Advance and Unbound as shared picks) and Doctrines chosen at character creation, each with its own gain triggers and specialized Holds and Releases. Momentum works as pressure: Holds are passives active at a threshold, Releases are spent. 'Attempting' a Momentum-generating action means taking the action, whatever the outcome. Six Doctrines: Breaker, Vanguard (renamed from Juggernaut), Bulwark, Warlord, Brawler, and a sixth still to find (Dread rejected). Abilities from another Doctrine can be learned at 2 repertoire slots each, up to a limit. Holds at maximum unlock later (ability tree). Taking two Doctrines is excluded for now and logged as Player Feedback entry 4. Open: a smaller Momentum maximum, slower gains (possibly Doctrine users gain only from their Doctrine's triggers), the Might threshold for choosing a Doctrine, and the cross-Doctrine limit.

**Momentum pool, gains and access (Established 2026-10-18; numbers Draft; Doctrine access and the cross-Doctrine limit superseded 2026-10-21, while the maximum and Hold thresholds stand).** Maximum Momentum = Might tens digit − 2 (Might 50: 3, 60: 4, 75: 5, 100: 8). A Doctrine requires Might 60. Doctrine users gain Momentum only from their Doctrine's triggers (Proposed frequency: once per round); characters without a Doctrine use the universal gain list (grapple 1, hit 1, knock Prone 1, shove 1, drop a foe to 0 HP 2); the once-per-turn limit is superseded 2026-10-21 by the 3-per-round cap. At most one ability from another Doctrine, costing 2 repertoire slots. Hold thresholds (Established 2026-10-18): Hold I at 2 Momentum, available with the Doctrine; Hold II at 4 Momentum, unlocked at Might 70; Hold at maximum later through the ability tree.

**Momentum Doctrines (roster Established 2026-10-18: seven Doctrines; every gain, Hold and Release below is Proposed).** Superseded 2026-10-21: Doctrines are dissolved and gains are additive (see above); this table is now a pool of Path ability drafts, filed in the Ability Catalog. Hold I needs 2 Momentum; Hold II needs 4 Momentum and Might 70.

Brawler grants Arripe (formerly Seize; renamed 2026-10-21) from creation (Established 2026-10-18; superseded 2026-10-21: Seize becomes a standalone Momentum ability): your Grab can be made as a strike, an unarmed attack that deals its damage and leaves the target Grabbed on a hit. Brawlers unlock Pin at Might 70, alongside Hold II (Established 2026-10-18). Sims: a Might-60 Brawler with Arripe wins 22% against the sword Duelist with fists, 37% with an off-hand dagger, and 52% as a pair in 2v2; at Might 70 with Pin, 64% / 59% / 58% (a plain Might-70 Duelist beats a Might-60 one 71%, so most of that jump is Might itself). Ironstring and Hurler were first simulated on 2026-10-18 (see the movement and range findings); cover still has no rule.

**Doctrine sims (Proposed findings, 2026-10-18; Harry +1, Quick Blade on).** Each Doctrine user is compared with the same build at the same Might without a Doctrine, which gains from the universal list and spends on Crushing Blows. As drafted, Doctrines come out slightly behind: Breaker Duelist 46–48%, Bulwark Defender 45–46%, a Warlord pair 49% in 2v2. Reasons: Crushing Blows as written (+1 damage per point, no limit) is a better sink than any Hold I, so spending beats holding; Bulwark gains only about 2 Momentum per fight from Parry and Block; Warlord gains only about 0.6, because an ally has to hit after the Warlord in the same round; Breaker's gain dries up once a foe's Guard is broken, and its Release (overflow carries to HP) never fired, because Committed Strike already does that. Tested fixes: Crushing Blows limited to 1 point per attack and Guard damage only (Breaker mirror 5.7 to 6.0 rounds); Bulwark also gains by ending its turn Warded (gains about 4 per fight, Bulwark 50%); Warlord also gains by hitting a foe an ally hit this round (51%). Breaker stays at 47–49% even with Hammerfall on every hit or gains from hits on a broken Guard, so its kit needs rework. Starting Doctrine users at 1 Momentum mostly helps Bulwark (53%; 59% at 2). Momentum overall is now worth only a few points in a duel (a Breaker against an opponent with no Momentum uses wins 52%).

**Adopted 2026-10-18 and retested:** Crushing Blows at 1 Momentum per attack, Guard damage only (Established); Bulwark's 'end your turn Warded' gain and Warlord's either-order gain (adopted into their drafts); Breaker reworked (gains all fight, Hold I Shattered, Release Ruin). Results against the same build without a Doctrine: Breaker Duelist 50% (Might 70: 50%), Breaker Bruiser 50%, Breaker in 2v2 48%; Bulwark Defender 50%; Warlord pair 49%. Shattered denies about one Rally per duel. Ruin fires rarely at Might 60 (about once every eight duels), because spending it drops the Breaker below Hold I. All three Doctrines now sit at parity with going without one in single fights.

**Harry and light weapons (Proposed findings, 2026-10-18):** Harry +1 instead of +2 lengthens fights slightly (Duelist mirror 6.0 to 6.1, Defender 9.2 to 9.7) and moves heavy and armored matchups by 2 points or less, but light weapons lose about 3 points (dagger Skirmisher 37% to 36%, rapier 29% to 28%). Light weapons Harrying at +2 while others get +1 gives dagger 39% and rapier 30%; at +3, 43% and 33%. A Harrying light blade that can't be Parried changes nothing against the light-armored Duelist, who Dodges. Letting light (Finesse) weapons Harry and Ward with the same Minor action gives dagger 45% and rapier 35% (dagger mirror 6.7 rounds); combined with light Harry +2 it overshoots (dagger 53%, rapier 47%).

**Harry and Quick Blade (2026-10-18).** Harry adds +1 Guard damage (Established; was +2); boosts such as Breaker's Hammerfall add to that base. Quick Blade (approved for simulation, not yet final): with a light (Finesse) weapon, your Minor action can be both Harry and Ward. Note for later: Harry and Ward together might instead belong to a dual-wielding fighting style (a light weapon in the off hand), rather than to every light weapon.

**Universal uses (updated 2026-10-21): Crushing Blows, Driving Strike and Shoulder the Blow, plus the universal Crest Release Overwhelm; Relentless Advance is now a Path ability**. Its main use is fueling Sword Path abilities and future maneuvers. Primary users: weapon fighters and brawlers; archers barely use it.

### Focus (Acuity)

**Refine (Established 2026-10-17):** after you see your attack roll and before the outcome is declared, you may spend 1 Focus to move the result 1 toward a critical result: down onto exactly the target's Defense (Precision), or up to beat it by 10 (Overwhelming). A roll that misses by 1 can be lifted onto exactly Defense, becoming a hit and a Precision crit. No action. At most 1 Focus per attack. More points or more movement per point are reserved for abilities such as Improved and Superior Critical. Focus moves attack rolls only; it can't move saves or d100 tests (Established 2026-10-17, can be revisited later). This limits roll adjustment only: the Focus Actions below stay in the design.

**Focus Actions** (1 Focus each, Draft names): **Refine** (attack steering, Established above), **Analyze** (learn about a creature), **Read** (discern intent), **Seize** (act at the top of initiative), **Spot** (detect a hidden creature), **Recover** (remove Surprised), **Pierce** (see through a Glamour), **Hone** (refine a spell). Pool size (Established 2026-10-17): Acuity tens digit; recovery by the Short Rest morning d100 roll (Established).

**Focus Actions rules text (2026-10-17; each row gives its status).** Each costs 1 Focus. None of them moves a roll.

| Action | Cost and timing | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analyze | Minor action | (Established 2026-10-17) Choose a creature you have seen attack or defend this encounter. Ask the GM one question below; the GM answers truthfully in plain description, never numbers. How hard is it to land a blow? How much fight is left in it (fresh, fraying or breaking)? What does it fear or shrug off? What will its blows do to me (damage type, crit effect or most dangerous attack)? See the answer guide below the table. |
| Read | No action, right after you succeed on an Insight test against a creature you are watching | (Established 2026-10-17; wording Draft) Ask the GM one more question about it, answered truthfully in plain words: Does it believe what it just said? What does it want from me? What is it afraid I'll learn? You learn that something is hidden and what it touches, never the secret itself. Social only; a combat version (learn a foe's intent for its next turn) is reserved as a later Acuity upgrade. |

**Analyze answer guide.** Fight left (Established 2026-10-17): **fresh**, Guard above half; **fraying**, Guard at half or less; **breaking**, Guard gone and blows reaching the body. How hard to hit (Established 2026-10-17, from Defense): a soft target (10 or less), guarded (11–13), armored (14–16), nearly impervious (17 or more); the GM describes it in the fiction ("its hide turns blades; you'll need a clean strike"). The knowledge isn't automatically permanent: what is learned about one creature needn't hold for its kind.

### Grit (Vigor)

You chose a static pool focused on survival and conditions, not damage reduction. Proposed universal uses (1 Grit each):

- **Push Through** (Established 2026-10-17, reworked): when a Major condition would be applied to you, spend 1 Grit to suffer its lesser form instead: Stunned becomes Dazed, Restrained becomes Grabbed. No action or reaction. Doesn't work on Petrified or on Incapacitated from unconsciousness or Dying. (Replaces 'delay a condition until the end of your next turn', which now duplicates Endure.)
- **Shake It Off**: spend 1 Grit to end one Minor condition: Exposed, Staggered, Flat-Footed, Dazed, Frightened, Slowed, Impaled or Seized (list updated 2026-10-16). It can't end Bleeding (Endure or binding), Sundered, Prone (stand up), Grabbed (break free with Wrestle), Shaken (Clarity's), or any Major condition. Timing (Established 2026-10-17): use it when you gain the condition or at the start of your turn; no action or reaction; once per round.
- **Stay Standing**: at 0 HP, stay conscious until the end of your next turn. Once per encounter.
- **Endure** (Established 2026-10-16): spend 1 Grit to suspend all effects of one condition (an Injury, your Fatigue, or Bleeding, including Bleeding's HP loss). Endure doesn't end the condition: a condition with a duration keeps counting down, and if any remains when Endure ends, it takes effect again. Each condition needs its own Grit, so two conditions take two uses. Endure takes no action and is used on your turn; it lasts until the end of your next turn. It also covers Poisoned, Burning, Caustic and Frostbitten. (Details Established 2026-10-17.)
- **Bear the Wound** (Established 2026-10-17): during a Brief or Short Rest, spend 1 Grit to set aside one Injury's or Lingering wound's effects until the end of your next Short Rest. It heals no faster: it still needs treatment and Long Rests, a Maiming wound still turns if untreated, and a new Injury at that location ends it. It doesn't work on a Ruin or Loss. Endure remains the in-fight tool.
- **Rally**: Major action, Committed; costs 1 Grit. At the start of your next turn regain 5 Guard. Broken if a hit damages your HP before then. Once per encounter. (Moved here from the universal combat actions 2026-10-16.)

**Grit recovery (Established 2026-10-16):** on each Short Rest, roll d100; if the result is equal to or under your Vigor, regain 1 Grit, up to your pool. This replaces the older rule that a character with Vigor at least half the attribute's maximum gains 1 free Grit each day. Rally (see Grit's uses) costs 1 Grit.

Open: whether a +0 Vigor character gets a minimum of 1 Grit.

**Grit test on the calibrated combat numbers (Proposed, 2026-10-16):** pool = Vigor tens digit, morning d100 recovery, Rally and Stay Standing each 1 Grit. In an even duel (Vigor 50, 5 Grit each), unlimited Rally stretches fights to 11.2 rounds (90% end by 19); Rally once per fight gives 8.0, and adding Stay Standing once per fight gives 8.3, on target. Over a week of one fight a day with those limits, fights started with no Grit fall from 43% (Vigor 30) to 15% (Vigor 50) and 1% (Vigor 75), and the win rate rises from 36% to 47% to 55%. At two fights a day everyone runs dry often (32–69% of fights). Vigor barely matters inside a single duel (+10 Vigor wins 52%) but clearly matters across an expedition. Decided 2026-10-16 (Established): Rally and Stay Standing are each usable once per encounter. The morning recovery roll became Established on 2026-10-16; the pool size (Vigor tens digit) became Established on 2026-10-17. Push Through, Shake It Off and Endure are drafted above and tested below.

**Grit condition uses in the engine (Proposed findings, 2026-10-16):** Shake It Off and Endure added (Push Through has nothing to act on yet, since no weapon crit applies a Major condition). Simulated players keep enough Grit back for an unused Rally and Stay Standing. Win rates and fight length don't move by more than 2 points for any build. Shake It Off as a reaction is almost never usable (0.06–0.5 uses per fight), because the attack that crits you is usually the one you just Parried, Dodged or Blocked. With no action cost it is used about twice per fight and doubles Grit spent per fight (about 1.1 to 2.1). Endure is used about 0.7 times per fight against daggers, cuts Bleeding damage by about a quarter, and drops the dagger from 26% to 24% against the Duelist (2v2: 25% to 20%). Over a week, Shake It Off (no action) plus Endure raises fights started with no Grit at Vigor 50 from 5% to 16% at one fight a day, and from 34% to 45% at two. The conditions these uses counter barely matter in single fights yet, so the true value of Grit's condition uses waits on Injuries and multi-fight attrition.

**Daggers and Endure (Proposed findings, 2026-10-17):** with the approved Grit uses, the dagger Skirmisher wins 23% against the Duelist (2v2: 20%); with Endure switched off it would win 25% (26%), so Endure is not the main problem. The d4 is: the Duelist's own build with a dagger instead of a sword wins only 11%, and the unarmored Skirmisher build is weak in its own right (the same build in light armor wins 37%). Armor's Guard Reduction takes a large share of a d4 hit. Options tested against the Duelist (unarmored / light-armored Skirmisher): d6 die 37% / 53%; short-blade hits that damage HP add Bleeding 26% / 39%; Overwhelming at beat-by-7 25% / 39%; ignoring Guard against Prone, Grabbed or Flat-Footed foes 24% / 37%; reducing armor GR by 1 36% / 53% (plate-and-shield Defender 5%); ignoring armor GR entirely 36% / 51% (Defender 1% to 20%, medium-armored Macer 12% to 39%, Spearman 12% to 38%, Bruiser 10% to 17%). Adopted 2026-10-17 as the Misericorde rule (Established): short blades ignore the armor's Guard Reduction; Parry still applies. Endure stays as written: binding, Tend and Endure are Bleeding's intended counters.

**Focus steering in the engine (Proposed findings, 2026-10-17):** pool = Acuity tens digit, Short Rest morning d100 ≤ Acuity regains 1. Steering a roll that already hits onto a crit (up to 2 points) raises crits from about a third of hits to about half, but changes no win rate except the dagger's (+4), because most crit conditions barely matter in a single fight yet. Letting Focus also lift a near miss up to exactly Defense (a hit and a Precision crit) is where the power is: in a mirror where only one side has Focus, up to 2 points per attack wins 58–66% and spends 1.4–3 Focus per fight; up to 1 point per attack wins 53–60% and spends 0.5–1.1; no cap wins 60–72% and drains the pool every fight. Over a week (Acuity 40 / 55 / 70), up to 1 point per attack leaves fights started with no Focus at 7% / 2% / 0% at one fight a day and 32% / 19% / 4% at two; up to 2 points gives 28% / 17% / 4% and 53% / 45% / 27%. When everyone has Focus, matchups barely move (dagger 36% to 38–42%). Adopted 2026-10-17 as Refine (Established): no action, after seeing your attack roll and before the outcome, spend 1 Focus to move it 1 toward a critical result, including lifting a miss by 1 onto exactly Defense; at most 1 Focus per attack, leaving 'Stronger Focus' (more points or more movement per point) to Improved and Superior Critical.

### Tempo (Poise)

You chose a generated, cyclical resource: built by repositioning, lost when your rhythm breaks. Gain 1 when you reposition relative to an enemy, move before and after attacking, evade an attack or effect, move through a dangerous area unharmed, or use an ability that changes your position (once per trigger per turn). Lose it by staying stationary, being restrained or knocked prone; an alternative is losing 1 at the start of each turn.

**Steps** (default Tempo uses, names proposed):

| Cost | Step | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Off Step | Flat-Foot an enemy |
| 1 | Quick Step | Move up to 10 ft |
| 1 | Sure Step | Ignore difficult terrain |
| 1 | Ghost Step | Move through an occupied space |
| 3 | Fleet Step | Gain Fleet of Foot |

Tempo is also the main tool for manipulating initiative (En Avant, Ensemble). The Steps table and gain rules in this paragraph's neighborhood are superseded by the Established Tempo rules of 2026-10-17.

**Creator's latest Tempo draft (Draft, supplied 2026-10-17; supersedes the gain, loss and Step text above).** Tempo is an accrued resource: dancing and flowing through the commotion of a battlefield is its language. Maximum Tempo = Poise modifier + 3 (written as Agility, the attribute's old name). You lose 1 Tempo when you don't attempt actions that would gain Tempo, and 1 at the end of each turn; you lose all Tempo when Incapacitated or reduced to 0 HP. Gain 1 Tempo per round from any one of: moving before and after an attack; successfully evading a melee attack; passing through and leaving a threatened area; performing an Evasive Maneuver. Spend: Off Beat, Flat-Foot an enemy (1); Chassé, move up to 10 ft (1); Promenade, pass through difficult terrain (1); Croisé, pass through an occupied space (1); Full Out, gain Fleet of Foot (3); Dégagé, Disengage (1); En Avant, act first in initiative (2). Design goal: many small actions that compound into larger ones, an ebb and flow, a dance. Under review: see the Tempo discussion of 2026-10-17.

**Tempo (Established 2026-10-17; numbers Draft).** Tempo is an accrued resource drawn from Poise: dancing and flowing through the commotion of a battlefield. Maximum Tempo = Poise modifier + 3. *First Position:* you start each encounter with 1 Tempo. *Beats:* gain at most 1 Tempo on your turn (move before and after an attack, or pass through and leave a threatened area) and 1 off your turn (the first time each round a melee attack against you misses, Dodge included), so up to 2 per round. *The ebb:* at the end of your turn, if you gained no Tempo this round, lose 1. You lose all Tempo when Incapacitated or reduced to 0 HP, and it fades when the encounter ends. *Spending:* Steps cost 1 and you may take any number per turn; Flourishes cost 2 and need Poise 60 or more. Everyone with Tempo knows Chassé; every other use is a repertoire pick (Poise tens digit − 2 slots, minimum 1).

**First Position and Tempo pricing (Established 2026-10-17):** every character with Tempo starts each encounter with 1 Tempo (the dancer begins poised). Every Step costs 1 Tempo and every Flourish costs 2 (En Avant, Full Out, Coda, and the candidate Flourishes Pirouette, Pas de Deux and Ensemble). The rest of Tempo was approved the same day (see the Tempo rules above).

**Expression repertoires (Established 2026-10-17; numbers Draft; a retraining rule is still Proposed: a Long Rest of practice swaps one use):** for Expressions with long menus, characters don't know every use. Proposed shape: the attribute sets how many uses you can hold (Tempo repertoire = Poise tens digit − 2, minimum 1: Poise 50 holds 3, 60 holds 4, 75 holds 5); new uses are learned with XP or with mentor training during a Long Rest; Flourishes require Poise 60 or more. Everyone with Tempo knows the beats, the ebb and Chassé, so Tempo always has an outlet. Proposed scope: Tempo and Momentum; Grit's uses and the Focus Actions stay universal. XP costs wait on the XP economy. Candidate additions to the Tempo menu: Échappé, Plié, Pirouette (defense); Pas de Deux, Ensemble (support).

**Tempo in the engine (Proposed findings, 2026-10-17):** the baseline beats and ebb, with Off Beat, Coda, Pirouette, Plié and En Avant modeled (movement-only uses can't be). An unarmored Poise-60 Skirmisher gains about 4.8 Tempo per duel (3.8 from attacking on the move, 1.0 from being missed) but holds only about 1 Tempo on an average attack, and 3 or more on just 13% of attacks: duels last about 6 rounds, Committed Strike and Rally turns trigger the ebb, and a third of the fight passes before a flourish is affordable. Coda lands about once every five duels. Win rates barely move: the dagger Skirmisher goes from 38% to 39–42% depending on its repertoire, the rapier stays at 27–29%, and giving every build its legal repertoire changes no matchup by more than 1 point. So Tempo doesn't feed Poise dominance, but in single fights it is mostly flavor. Variants: starting each fight with 1 Tempo raises the average to 1.4; Coda at 2 doubles Codas; both together give about one Coda every two duels (dagger 40%); starting with 2 gives 28% of attacks at 3+ Tempo (dagger 41%).

**Tempo as Defense (Proposed findings, 2026-10-17):** with First Position and 2-cost Flourishes, a Poise-60 Skirmisher holds 3+ Tempo on about a quarter of the attacks against it (about 45% if it never spends). A passive +1 Defense at 3+ Tempo (light or no armor) moves the dagger Skirmisher by 0–2 points; +1 at 2+ and +2 at 4+ by up to 2; the Duelist mirror doesn't move. Holding Tempo for Defense without spending wins 38%, against 40% for spending on Off Beat, Coda, Pirouette and Plié, so holding and spending are close and the choice is real. Candidate: In the Flow, a repertoire Step-tier passive: while you hold 3 or more Tempo and wear light or no armor, +1 Defense.

### Fortune (Providence)

Fortune changes outcomes; Focus changes numbers. Proposed uses: **Second Chance** (reroll your d20), **Twist of Fate** (force another creature to reroll), **Lucky Break** (a small beneficial coincidence, GM approval), **Narrow Escape** (turn a critical hit against you into a normal hit). Open: how Fortune relates to the existing Providence-in-saves rule, and whether it becomes the resource everyone wants.

### Cognition (Sagacity)

Cognition is not what you know; it is how much you can maintain at once. Each archetype has its own base, and Sagacity modifier adds to it, so a fighter never needs Sagacity to function. It expands prepared formulas, maintained hexes and wards, glamours, traps, and battle plans. Example: Fighter base 1 battle plan + Cognition 2 = 3; Alchemist base 4 formula slots + 2 = 6.

### Authority (Presence) — Established

Authority is not spent. Each archetype has a Base Authority; your Presence modifier increases it. It sets the number, strength or scale of what you command: a Sorcerer's tolerance for instability, a Warlock's bound entities and pacts, a Veilwalker's spirits and dreams, a leader's affected allies. There is no universal Authority action list; specific abilities say how they use it.
