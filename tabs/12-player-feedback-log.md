# Player Feedback Log

*A reference list of systems to put in front of players for feedback. A system goes into this log only when the creator says so; nothing is added on its own. Each entry points back to the Design Bible section that holds the full rules.*

## How this log works

- **Adding an entry:** tell Claude to log a system for players. Claude writes the entry from the template below, copies the system's current status from the Design Bible, and updates the index.
- **Statuses** match the Design Bible: Established, Draft, Proposed or Open. Open questions belong here too; they are often the most useful thing to ask players.
- **Entries are snapshots.** Each shows the system as it stood on its added date. If the rules change, update the entry and add a dated line to its history. Never delete earlier feedback.
- **Feedback and outcomes** are filled in after sessions. A decision that changes a system is made in the Design Bible and its Decision log, not here.
- **Statuses are never promoted here.** They follow the Design Bible, and a promotion needs the creator's explicit approval.
- **Wording is for players:** plain language, no designer shorthand, and a short example at the table.

## Entry template

Copy this for each new entry, numbered in order.

> **### \[Number\]. \[System name\]**
>
> - **Status:** Established, Draft, Proposed or Open, as of the added date
> - **Added:** date · **Design Bible section:** where the full rules live
> - **Summary for players:** two or three plain sentences on what it is and why it exists
> - **At the table:** one short example of it in play
> - **Questions for players:** two to four specific questions
> - **Feedback received:** date, who, what was said. Leave blank until sessions happen
> - **Outcome:** what changed, if anything, and which Decision log row records it
> - **History:** dated changes to the entry

### 3. Natural 20 and natural 1 on saves

- **Status:** Rejected for now (2026-10-15). Saves have no automatic results; the creator wants player input before deciding for good. Attacks follow the same rule (decided 2026-10-16): a natural 20 does not always hit and a natural 1 does not always miss, so ask players about both together.
- **Design Bible section:** Core resolution, Save DC
- **Summary for players:** When something forces you to save, you roll d20 + your save bonus against a DC. Right now a save has no automatic results: a natural 20 can still fail and a natural 1 can still succeed. A save bonus of −2 against a DC of 20 cannot succeed at all, and a +10 bonus against a DC of 10 cannot fail.
- **The rule considered:** a natural 20 always saves and a natural 1 always fails. That guarantees every save a 5% chance either way, matching the skill rule that 01 always succeeds and 100 always fails.
- **At the table:** "The lich's gaze is DC 20 and your Courage is −2, so only a natural 20 can save you." Without the rule: "You cannot resist it."
- **Questions for players:**
  1. Should a natural 20 always save, even against an overwhelming threat? Should a natural 20 always hit, or a natural 1 always miss, on an attack?
  2. Should a natural 1 always fail, even for a character who has mastered that save?
  3. Does a guaranteed 5% chance make the game feel fairer, or does it undercut how deadly the world should feel?
- **Feedback received:**
- **Outcome:**
- **History:** 2026-10-15, entry created after the creator rejected the rule pending player input.

### 5. Momentum ability names

- **Status:** Established working names (2026-10-21); players vote on the alternatives below.
- **Added:** 2026-10-21 · **Design Bible section:** Expressions, Momentum (Might)
- **Summary for players:** Momentum is the pressure a fighter builds by hitting, shoving and grappling, then spends on powerful moves. Several of those moves have new or working names. Each has a working name, and we'd like you to vote on the alternatives.
- **The abilities:** *Shoulder the Blow* (working name; no alternatives yet): spend 1 Momentum to take 1 less Guard damage from an attack. *Feed the Wolves* (working name): after you shove or drive an enemy, an ally next to it attacks it at once. Alternatives: *Into the Teeth*, *Hammer and Anvil*, *Serve Them Up*, *Throw Them to the Pack*. Hard on the Heels (working name): after you throw a weapon at an enemy, run at it and strike it in melee. Alternatives: Chase the Blade, Run It Down. Overwhelm (working name; suggestions welcome): when your Momentum peaks, pour all of it into one crushing hit or into steadying yourself.
- **At the table:** "I slam him back with Driving Strike, and that's Feed the Wolves: Mara, he's yours."
- **Questions for players:**
  1. Which names for the ally-attack move and the throw-and-charge move fit Asloria best?
  2. Which is easiest to call out mid-fight?
  3. Does Shoulder the Blow say clearly what it does?
- **Feedback received:**
- **Outcome:**
- **History:** 2026-10-21, entry created when the creator put the alternatives to a player vote. 2026-10-21, Hard on the Heels added with Chase the Blade and Run It Down as alternatives. 2026-10-21, Overwhelm added as a working name open to player suggestions.

### 6. Negative Providence and Fortune

- **Status:** Open question. The current rule is Established (2026-10-24): Providence substitution uses and the Fortune pool both equal the Providence modifier, to a minimum of 0, so a negative modifier simply gives nothing. The creator wants player input on whether a negative score should do more than that.
- **Added:** 2026-10-24 · **Design Bible section:** Core resolution, Providence in saves; Attribute Expressions, Fortune (Providence)
- **Summary for players:** Providence is your character's fate, and it gives you two things: a few chances per week to lean on luck when you make a save, and a small pool of Fortune for rerolls and lucky breaks. Both grow with your Providence modifier. Every character starts with Providence 40, which is a −1 modifier, so unless you raise it, you begin with neither. Right now a low score just means no luck in your favour. The question is whether it should also mean luck against you.
- **The idea considered:** the GM gains ill fortune to use against a character in proportion to how negative their modifier is. For example, a character at −1 might give the GM one chance per Long Rest to make that character reroll a success, or to have a small misfortune befall them. This is only an illustration; no mechanic has been chosen.
- **At the table:** "Your Providence is −2, so this week the world owes you two bad turns. Your bowstring snaps on that killing shot; roll again."
- **Questions for players:**
  1. Should a low Providence ever work against your character, or is having no luck to spend punishment enough?
  2. If it did, would it feel like fitting grim fate, or like being punished for a starting number most characters share?
  3. Would you rather the GM's ill fortune be a specific reroll, a narrative misfortune, or something else?
  4. Would the threat make you more likely to raise Providence, and is that a choice you'd enjoy making?
- **Feedback received:**
- **Outcome:**
- **History:** 2026-10-24, entry created at the creator's request after the minimum of 0 was set for Providence uses and the Fortune pool.
