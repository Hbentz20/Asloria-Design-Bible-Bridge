# Skill Tests

*Adopted 2026-10-13 and written as it would appear in the GM's guide. Parts marked Draft or Open are still being worked on; see the last section. Saves returned to the d20 on 2026-10-14, so this chapter now covers skills only. Updated 2026-10-15 for the 0–100 attribute scale and the new attribute and save names.*

## The Two Dice

Asloria uses two dice, and each has its own job. Knowing which to reach for is simple once you see the line between them.

**Roll a d20 in combat and whenever danger comes for you.** Attacks are rolled against the target's Defense, initiative is rolled each round, and saves are rolled against a DC when a poison, a blast or a curse tries to take hold. These moments are fast and tense, and the d20's broad swings suit them.

**Roll a d100 when you rely on what you have learned.** Whenever a character uses a skill (climbing a crumbling wall, recalling a forbidden rite, talking a guard out of raising the alarm), they make a **test** on percentile dice.

A single action never asks for both dice. A shield-bash is an attack, and if it can knock the target down, the target's save against it is also a d20. Skills are used for their own actions: searching, climbing, persuading, recalling.

> *GM's note:* If a player asks "which die?", ask them back: "Are you trying to get past someone, or are you testing yourself?" That answer is almost always right.

## Making a Test

Every skill is written on the character sheet as a percentage: the character's chance of success at a task of ordinary difficulty. To make a test, the player rolls percentile dice (two ten-sided dice, one for tens and one for ones) and reads the result from 01 to 100. A roll of 00 on both dice counts as 100.

**If the roll is equal to or lower than the character's adjusted percentage, the test succeeds.** If it is higher, the test fails.

There are no ranges of automatic success or failure, with one exception that applies to every test: **a roll of 01 always succeeds, and a roll of 100 always fails**, whatever the percentage. A character with 7% succeeds on 01 to 07; a character at –20% still has the 01. A character pushed above 100% still fails on a 100. Nobody is ever entirely safe, and nobody is ever entirely without hope.

On a skill test that matters, those same two rolls are also critical results: 01 is a Breakthrough and 100 is a Setback (see *Critical Results on Skills*).

### Difficulty

Before the roll, the GM decides how hard the task is. Difficulty does not add or subtract percentage points; it scales the character's chance. A character's sheet value is their chance at a Normal task, and harder tasks use a fraction of it. (Established 2026-10-14)

| Difficulty | Target number | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Full skill | Climbing a rough stone wall |
| Medium | Half skill | Climbing that wall in the rain |
| Hard | One third of skill | Climbing it in the rain with a wounded arm |
| Very Hard | One fifth of skill | Scaling a sheer cliff at night |

Round every fraction down. A roll of 01 still always succeeds and a roll of 100 still always fails, so a target that rounds to 0 leaves only the 01. (Rounding down: Established 2026-10-14.)

Circumstances do not change the character's percentage. Tools, conditions, help and abilities change the difficulty tier of the task instead, and the chart below gives the target (see *Tools, Conditions and Help* at the end of this tab). The only situational changes to the sheet number are Mental Wall and In the Zone (see *Critical Results on Skills*), and a specialization adds to the full skill before the fraction. (Established 2026-10-14.)

A roll of 100 always fails, so the best Normal target is 99. Quick reading: a roll equal to or under half your skill is a Medium success, one third a Hard success, one fifth a Very Hard success. One roll tells you the degree.

> *GM's note:* Call for a test only when failure would change the story. If a character has time, safety and the right tools, simply let them succeed. Tests carry weight in Asloria because they are reserved for moments that matter, and both improvement and critical results depend on that.

## Skills

A skill is something a person learns. Natural talent decides how quickly someone takes to it, but nobody becomes truly proficient without practice, instruction and hard experience. The skill formula reflects this: attributes provide a modest foundation, and training builds everything above it.

> **Skill % = (Attribute A + Attribute B) ÷ 5 + Training %**

Each skill names its two attributes (for example, Athletics uses Might and Vigor). Add the two scores and divide by five, rounding down, to find the character's talent; then add any Training they have earned. (Established 2026-10-15: dividing by five carries the old 1–10 formula onto the 0–100 scale with the same results.)

This means raw talent alone only goes so far. An ordinary person (50 and 50) has 20% in a skill they have never studied. A new character's strongest possible pair (60 and 60) gives 24%, two attributes raised to the XP ceiling of 75 give 30%, and even a legendary natural at 100 and 100 has only 40%. Mastery, the 85–95% of a veteran who has spent a lifetime at their craft, comes almost entirely from Training.

### Starting training

At character creation a character has four trained skills: two from their background and two of their choice. Each starts with **+20% Training**. All other skills start at 0% Training.

> *Designer's note:* Skills as a whole category (the skill list, starting Training, and the architecture around them) will be revisited to confirm they are what we are aiming for.

### Trained-only skills

Some knowledge cannot be guessed at. **Nature, Arcana, Occult, Medicine and History** cannot be tested at all without at least some Training in them. An untrained character simply does not know; the GM should describe what they see without explaining what it means.

### Specializations

Through advancement, a character gains specializations, each in a subskill of a skill they already have Training in (Tracking within Survival, say, or First Aid within Medicine). When a test uses that subskill, the specialization adds:

| Level | Specialization bonus |
| --- | --- |
| 1–4 | +10% |
| 5–10 | +15% |
| 11–20 | +20% |

*Open (2026-10-15): there are no character levels, so how specializations are gained, and when the bonus grows, will be set with the XP economy. The level bands above are placeholders until then.*

The specialization bonus is added to the character's full skill **before** the difficulty fraction is applied (Established 2026-10-14). A Tracking specialist with a +15% specialization and Survival at 65% reaches 80, so a Hard test uses one third of 80: 26.

### Gambling

Gambling breaks the usual pattern, because fortune favours the bold of any stripe: **Gambling % = (Providence + the character's highest other attribute) ÷ 5 + Training %.**

> *GM's note:* Because Training dominates, two characters with very different attributes can be equally capable after years of work. Talent shows up as a head start, and as the reason one apprentice outpaces another, never as a substitute for practice.

## Earning Improvement

In Asloria, skills grow because they are used under pressure. A character who picks locks in the dark while guards approach learns more than one who practises on a bench, and the rules reward that.

> *Status: Established 2026-10-24.* The tick-and-roll structure, the end-of-session timing and the current pace are adopted. Progress is deliberately slow, which suits a long campaign.

### Ticking a skill

When a character **succeeds at a skill test that matters**, a test where failure carried a real cost, the player places a tick beside that skill. A skill can earn only **one tick per session**, however many times it is used. The GM may refuse a tick for a test that had no real stakes, was attempted only to earn a tick, or simply repeated an earlier success.

### The improvement roll

At the end of each session, the player makes an improvement roll for every ticked skill, then erases the ticks.

1. Roll d100.
2. If the roll is **higher than the skill's current percentage**, the character has learned something new: add **1d4% to Training**.
3. If the roll is equal to or lower, the lesson was smaller but not wasted: add **1% to Training**.

Use the skill's normal value for this roll, ignoring specializations and temporary bonuses or penalties.

This creates natural diminishing returns. A novice improves quickly because almost every roll beats their low percentage; a master improves slowly because they have little left to learn. On average a ticked skill gains about 1.8% at 46%, 1.5% at 70% and 1.2% at 90%. Because a tick needs a success, a character who uses a skill seriously in most sessions will carry it from a starting 36–44% to around 90% over something like 45–60 sessions at a typical table (faster where most tests are Normal, slower where most are Hard), and every step of that climb will be visible on the sheet.

### Learning from others

Use is not the only teacher. During a Long Rest, a character who studies under a mentor, or works from proper texts, may make **one extra improvement roll for one skill**. If the skill is one they have no Training in, they instead gain their first **+10% Training**. This is the only way to begin a trained-only skill such as Medicine or Occult after character creation.

Mentors are rare in a world the gods have left. They ask for coin, favors, service or worse, and the GM should make finding one part of the story.

There are no character levels, and Training never comes from general advancement: skills grow through use and instruction alone. Whether the XP pool can ever buy Training will be settled with the XP economy (Open).

> *GM's note:* Ticks are the engine of advancement, so guard them. Ask "what happens if this fails?" before every test. If the answer is "nothing much", there is no test and no tick.

## Critical Results on Skills

Once in a long while, a skill test produces something more than success or failure: a flash of mastery, or a moment of humiliation that lingers. Each has a 1% chance, which makes them rare enough to be remembered.

Critical results apply **only to skill tests that matter** (the same tests that can earn a tick). On a test with no real stakes, a 01 or a 100 is simply a success or a failure.

### Breakthrough (a roll of 01)

The test succeeds, whatever the character's percentage, and the character glimpses something deeper in their craft. The player chooses one:

- **Insight.** Add 1d4% to the skill's Training permanently, immediately. This is in addition to any tick the success earns.
- **In the Zone.** The skill gains +10% until the character's next Long Rest.

### Setback (a roll of 100)

The test fails, whatever the character's percentage, and the failure gets under their skin. The character suffers a **Mental Wall**: the skill takes −10% until whichever comes first:

- the character's next Long Rest, or
- the character succeeds at a test with that skill in a moment that matters.

A Mental Wall never reduces Training. The character has not lost their skill, only their confidence, and the second ending gives them a way to win it back. Multiple Setbacks on the same skill do not stack; a second one simply restarts the Mental Wall.

A character may spend Fortune's **Second Chance** to reroll a Setback. The new roll stands, whatever it shows.

> *GM's note:* Describe Breakthroughs and Setbacks in the fiction. A Breakthrough might be a lock that suddenly makes sense, a forgotten verse surfacing at the perfect moment. A Setback might be trembling hands after a pick snaps, or a name that will not come. Players remember these moments long after the numbers fade.

## A Note on Saves

Saves are not tests. They roll on the d20 against a DC, so that an attack and the save it forces never need two kinds of dice. There are six saves: Reflex, Fortitude, Will, Footing, Courage and Scrutiny. Each adds the modifiers of its two attributes, to a maximum of +7 from attributes, and Providence may stand in for one of them (see the main rules).

## Converting Older d20 Numbers

Much of Asloria's earlier material was written for d20 skill checks. This conversion applies to skill checks only; saves and save DCs stay on the d20. Until each rule is rewritten, convert it at the table with one rule of thumb: **one point on a d20 equals 5% on a test.**

| Older rule | Convert to |
| --- | --- |
| A flat bonus or penalty (+2, −1) | 5% per point (+10%, −5%) |
| A fixed DC | (15 − DC) × 5%. DC 14 becomes +5%; DC 18 becomes −15% |
| A rising DC (+3 per use) | −15% per use |
| +1d4 to a skill check | +10% |
| Advantage | +20% (and see the design pillar on avoiding advantage) |
| Disadvantage | −20% |

A few existing traits, converted:

- **Stonecunning** (Dwarves): on tests about the origin of stonework, add 10% per point of positive Sagacity modifier.
- **Revenant Elegy**: its DC 14 checks become Occult tests at +5%. Its Courage save stays on the d20, at DC 10 + the caster's Presence + Sagacity modifiers.

## An Example of Play

Hask is a new sellsword built with the quick-start array: Might 60, Vigor 50, Poise 45, Acuity 40, Providence 40, Presence 40, Sagacity 35. Athletics (Might + Vigor) is one of his trained skills, so his sheet reads **Athletics 42%** (talent (60 + 50) ÷ 5 = 22%, plus 20% Training). Investigation (Acuity + Sagacity) is not: he has only his talent, **Investigation 15%** ((40 + 35) ÷ 5).

Fleeing a burning granary, Hask reaches a wall slick with rain. The GM calls it Hard, so the target is a third of his 42%: 14. The player rolls 12, a success, and Hask hauls himself over as the roof gives way. The climb mattered, so Athletics gets a tick.

Later, trapped in the cellars with the fire spreading above, Hask searches the walls for another way out. The GM calls it Normal: 15%. The player rolls 01, a **Breakthrough**. Hask notices the draft tugging at the torch flame and finds the hidden coal chute behind a rotten board. The player chooses Insight, and Hask's Investigation Training rises from nothing to 3%: the first thing he has ever truly learned about searching a room.

At the end of the session, the player makes Athletics' improvement roll. They need to beat 42 and roll 63, so Hask has learned something real: 1d4 comes up 3, his Training rises to 23%, and his sheet now reads **Athletics 45%**. It is a small step, but he earned it on a wet wall with a fire at his back.

## Status and Open Questions

What has been settled for this chapter, and what is still being worked on.

**Decided:** skill checks are d100 tests (2026-10-13); 01 always succeeds and 100 always fails, as a Breakthrough or Setback when the test matters; +20% starting Training for all four starting skills; end-of-session improvement rolls; mentors and study as the route to new Training, with no Training from levels; Fortune may reroll a Setback. Saves and save DCs returned to the d20 (2026-10-14).

**Also decided (2026-10-14):** difficulty uses fractions of the character's skill: Normal full, Medium half, Hard one third, Very Hard one fifth. This replaces the additive difficulty ladder. Fractions round down, and specializations add to the full skill before the fraction.

**Also decided (2026-10-14):** Mental Wall (−10%) and In the Zone (+10%) stay numeric adjustments to the skill. They adjust the skill itself, so they are added to the full skill before the difficulty fraction, like a specialization. They are rare (only a 01 or 100 on a test that matters), clear on their own, and are the only situational changes to the sheet number.

**Also decided (2026-10-14):** tools, conditions and help change the difficulty tier rather than the percentage (see *Tools, Conditions and Help*). There is no Easy or Near Impossible tier.

Also decided (2026-10-15): attributes use the 0–100 scale and their new names (Might, Acuity, Vigor, Presence, Sagacity, Poise, Providence), and saves are six d20 saves (see A Note on Saves). Talent = (Attribute A + Attribute B) ÷ 5 (Established 2026-10-15), which keeps talent on the same 0–40% range as before.

**Still open:**

1. **Improvement pacing.** Resolved 2026-10-24: the current pace stands, since the campaign will be long.
2. **Focus on tests.** Revisit in detail whether Focus can affect d100 tests.
3. **Skill against save.** Decide how a d100 skill opposes a d20 save, as in Speak the Old Laws (an Occult check opposed by an undead's Will).
4. **Skills as a category.** Revisit the skill list and its surrounding architecture.
5. **The d20 audit.** Rewrite every ability that still refers to d20 skill checks.
6. **Specializations without levels.** Decide how specializations are gained and when the bonus grows, as part of the XP economy.
7. **Rerun the tuning.** Pacing rerun 2026-10-24 at the new starting values (two tests per session, 40/30/20/10 tier mix): reaching 90% takes about 66 sessions from 30%, 58 from 36%, 50 from 42% and 48 from 44% (Proposed figures), and that pace was accepted. The tier success-rate tables below still use the old 46% start.

**Added 2026-10-14 (Open):**

- **Easy and Near Impossible.** Resolved 2026-10-14: there is no Easy or Near Impossible tier. A task easier than Normal needs no roll, and a task beyond Very Hard leaves only the automatic 01 or is ruled impossible.
- **Converting older fixed DCs.** The d20 conversion table above still maps DCs and flat bonuses to percentage modifiers; rewrite it to use the four tiers and tier shifts.
- **Tier tuning.** First pass done (see Tier tuning results at the end of this tab). Tick rule settled 2026-10-14: ticks stay as written. Specializations add before the difficulty fraction (settled 2026-10-14).

**New open item (2026-10-14): stacking tier shifts.** Decide whether several conditions each shift the tier or only the worst applies, whether the total shift is capped (for example at two tiers), and what difficulty a helper's own test is made at. Which conditions hamper which skills belongs with the condition glossary. Deferred 2026-10-14: the cap stays unresolved until player feedback comes in, so it is a candidate for the Player Feedback Log once the skill system is ready.

## Tier tuning results (Proposed, 2026-10-14)

First check of the four-tier scale: success rates by skill, and how the tiers affect the tick-and-improvement pace. Simulated with 4,000 characters per scenario; a roll of 01 always succeeds and 100 always fails. Every number here is Proposed. These runs used 46%, the old typical starting skill; on the 0–100 scale a trained skill starts at 30–44%, so the figures need a rerun.

**Success rate by tier** (percent, new fractions against the old additive ladder)

| Full skill % | Normal | Medium | Hard (new) | Very Hard (new) | Hard (old, −25) | Very Hard (old, −50) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 20 | 10 | 6 | 4 | 1 | 1 |
| 46 | 46 | 23 | 15 | 9 | 21 | 1 |
| 70 | 70 | 35 | 23 | 14 | 45 | 20 |
| 95 | 95 | 47 | 31 | 19 | 70 | 45 |

**Sessions to climb one skill from 46% to 90%** (one tick per session, and a tick needs a success; the earlier "about thirty sessions" assumed a tick every session)

| GM mix of tiers | 1 test per session | 2 tests | 3 tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tick every session (baseline) | 30 | 30 | 30 |
| All Normal | 45 | 34 | 32 |
| 40% Normal, 30% Medium, 20% Hard, 10% Very Hard | 72 | 45 | 37 |
| 20% Normal, 30% Medium, 30% Hard, 20% Very Hard | 93 | 55 | 44 |
| All Hard | 137 | 77 | 57 |
| All Very Hard | 233 | 124 | 88 |

**Findings**

1. **The dead zone is gone.** A starting trained character (46%) has 9% on Very Hard instead of only the automatic 01, and a novice (20%) has 4–6% instead of 1%.
2. **Veterans lose reliability on hard tasks.** At 85%, Hard is 28% (it was 60%); at 95%, Very Hard is 19% (it was 45%). A master beats a novice by about 4.5 times at every tier, a constant multiple, so mastery never makes hard work routine. This is grim, but confirm it is intended.
3. **Harder tasks teach slower.** At 46%, an average session with two tests gives a tick 71% of the time at Normal, 41% at Medium, 28% at Hard and 17% at Very Hard. Attempting hard things is the slowest way to learn, which cuts against "skills grow because they are used under pressure."
4. **Progress slows more than "slightly."** A mixed table takes about 45 sessions instead of 30 with two tests per session.
5. **Breakthroughs cluster at low skill on hard tasks.** The 01 is both the automatic success and the Breakthrough. At 20%, a quarter of Very Hard successes are Breakthroughs; at 10%, half.

**Options for the tick rule (Proposed)**

- **A. Tick on the character's own skill.** A test that matters earns a tick when the roll is equal to or under the character's *full* skill, whatever the difficulty. The pace then matches the All Normal row (about 34 sessions at two tests), and attempting hard tasks no longer slows learning. Tune "slightly slower" with the improvement gain instead.
- **B. Leave ticks as they are.** Pace follows the GM's difficulty mix, 45–55 sessions for typical mixes.

**Decided 2026-10-14: Option B.** Ticks stay as written. A tick needs a success, so harder tasks teach more slowly and advancement is slow and grim, which is intended for a long campaign.

Not yet tested: attribute growth, the Mental Wall, and Focus on tests.

## Specialization check (2026-10-14)

A specialization adds +10% (levels 1–4), +15% (5–10) or +20% (11–20) when a test uses its subskill. The open question is whether that bonus is added to the character's full skill before the difficulty fraction, or to the target number after it. Success rates (percent) at Normal / Medium / Hard / Very Hard, with fractions rounded down:

| Stage | No specialization | Added before the fraction | Added after the fraction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Level 3–4 (skill 50, +10) | 50 / 25 / 16 / 10 | 60 / 30 / 20 / 12 | 60 / 35 / 26 / 20 |
| Level 5–10 (skill 65, +15) | 65 / 32 / 21 / 13 | 80 / 40 / 26 / 16 | 80 / 47 / 36 / 28 |
| Level 11+ (skill 80, +20) | 80 / 40 / 26 / 16 | 99 / 50 / 33 / 20 | 99 / 60 / 46 / 36 |

**Findings**

1. **Before the fraction** gives a steady edge of about 1.2–1.3 times at every tier. **After the fraction**, the edge grows with difficulty: about 1.2 times at Normal but 2.0–2.25 times at Very Hard.
2. After the fraction, a 60% specialist (+20) has 32% on Very Hard, against 18% for an unspecialized 90% master. Before the fraction, a 70% specialist (+20) just matches the master at 18%.
3. Normal targets cap at 99, so a +20 bonus on an 80% skill adds only 19, and any bonus above that is wasted at Normal. Its value shows at the harder tiers.
4. Specializations speed learning only a little. Improvement rolls ignore them, but a higher chance of success earns ticks sooner. Climbing 46% to 90% takes about 38–41 sessions before the fraction and 35–39 after, against 45 without (two tests per session, 40/30/20/10 tier mix, every test using the specialized subskill, so an upper bound).

**Decided 2026-10-14 (Established):** specializations are added to the character's full skill before the difficulty fraction. A specialist stays clearly better in their field without eclipsing a generalist master on the hardest tasks. The order of operations for help and conditions is still Proposed and is covered next.

## Tools, Conditions and Help: tier shifts (Established 2026-10-14)

Goal: the printed skill % changes only through advancement (Training, specializations, attribute increases). Tools, conditions and help never touch it; they change the difficulty tier of the task, and the threshold chart gives the target. The only exceptions are Mental Wall and In the Zone (see above). This replaces the earlier approach of adding circumstances to the skill, and settles Easy and Near Impossible: neither tier exists.

- **Conditions:** a condition that plausibly hampers the task makes it one tier harder.
- **Tools:** proper tools are the baseline and change nothing. Missing or poor tools make the task one tier harder, and some tasks cannot be attempted without them. Exceptional tools may make it one tier easier, which should be rare.
- **Help:** the helper makes their own test; a success makes your task one tier easier. Helpers do not stack beyond one step.
- **Abilities:** they state a tier shift instead of a percentage.
- **Limits:** a task cannot become easier than Normal (below that, no roll is needed). Beyond Very Hard, only the automatic 01 succeeds, or the GM rules the task impossible.

**What one step costs** (success percent)

| Tier | Skill 46% | Skill 70% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | 46 | 70 |
| Medium | 23 | 35 |
| Hard | 15 | 23 |
| Very Hard | 9 | 14 |

One step costs a lot at the top of the ladder (−23 points at 46%) and little at the bottom (−6), so conditions bite hardest on ordinary tasks. The GM judges whether a circumstance matters enough to shift a tier, which fits the rule to call for a test only when failure matters.

**Considered and not adopted:** adding tools, help and conditions to the full skill before the fraction in steps of 10%. Modelled 2026-10-14: it avoids the dead zone and stacks gently, but the printed number moves with the situation.
